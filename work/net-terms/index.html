01 — The challenge

Enterprises pay by invoice. Intuit charged a card.

Finance teams at larger businesses pay suppliers through purchase orders, invoices and scheduled payment runs. Intuit billed every subscription to a card or bank account on file, on the billing date. When a charge failed, access could stop.

How their finance teams pay
  1. Purchase order
  2. Invoice
  3. Approval
  4. Payment run, within terms
How Intuit billed
  1. Card or bank account on file
  2. Charged on the billing date
  3. A failed charge pauses access
$1.50

One enterprise customer paying $29K a year lost access over a failed $1.50 charge.

Key design challenge

How might enterprise customers pay Intuit by invoice, on approved terms, without changing how anyone else pays?

03 — Requirements

A credit product inside a shared widget.

11
Constraint

Products and flows load the same payment widget.

From TurboTax and QuickBooks to sales tools and the Partner Portal. Net Terms had to appear only for eligible customers and leave every other checkout as it was.

Two modes

Terms change in billing settings, never mid-purchase.

The widget opens in two modes. The mode decides what a customer can do with payment terms.

Manage mode

Billing settings

  • Apply for Net Terms
  • Track the application
  • Switch between Immediate and Net Terms
Transactional mode

Upgrade, downgrade, resubscribe, new orders

  • Pay with the terms on file
  • No applying
  • No switching terms
  • ExceptionOpen Net Terms invoices block a switch back to Immediate.
  • ExceptionIn collections, terms are locked. Only the payment method within them can change.

Decision 1

A declined application leads back to Immediate, not to Care

Chose
One next step in the widget: continue with Immediate Terms to complete the transaction or keep the account active.
Over
Sending declined customers to Care for an explanation.
Why
Intuit isn't obliged to share the reason, Care has no access to it, and the referral would only add calls.
After approval

The invoice becomes the payment method.

Once saved, the billing page shows Invoice Net 30 where the card used to be.

Decision 2

One consent sentence for cards and invoices, worked out with Legal

Chose
“By selecting [button], you authorize us to automatically charge/bill you … If you have an underlying contract specifying payment terms, we will charge/bill you in accordance with those terms.”
Over
A Net Terms version: “I authorize Intuit to invoice my account on mm/dd/yyyy and monthly after that … under the agreed Net Terms.”
Why
“Charge/bill” covers both ways to pay and defers to the customer's contract, so the same consent works in every flow and for accountants and partners alike.
Paying

Due dates and balances, one click from payment.

Billing history adds the due date, status and outstanding amount. Unpaid and partly paid invoices get a Make a payment action that opens Intuit's payment portal. Intuit Account payment history follows the same pattern.

Invoice

Terms at the top of every invoice.

Payment method, terms and the due date sit in the header, next to the invoice number and total. Accountant invoices add billing by client.

Emails

Eight emails, from application to credit limit.

Application updates, the monthly invoice, and alerts as usage nears the credit limit. Watermarked body copy was owned by FLP (Finance, Legal and Privacy).

05 — Reflection

Team, and what I learned.

Team
  • My contributionFlows for accountants, direct customers and partners: widget states, application, purchases on terms, billing history, invoices and emails; prototypes and handoff
  • Senior designerDesign direction and reviews
  • Product managerRequirements, scope and alignment with finance and legal teams
  • EngineeringWidget states, billing changes and integrations
What I learned

  • Design the in-between

    Review, decline and blocked states needed as much care as approval.

  • Rules before screens

    Agreeing when terms can change settled questions across every flow at once.

  • Copy is shared work

    Consent wording went through Legal, so words were reviewed as closely as layouts.
