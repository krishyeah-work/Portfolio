01 — The trip

Each city is a level.

You don't open lessons. You land in Barcelona, and every scene is a real errand: breakfast, the market, a haircut. Finish a city's scenes, earn Rep, and travel on to Madrid.

02 — Pictures first

You work the word out from the scene.

Lucía asks, “¿Una mesa para uno?” The English line keeps a blank where the new word goes, so you find it in the picture and tap the table. Translation is there, but only if you ask.

03 — Sound bridges

The languages you already speak help.

You start by picking the languages you know. When a new word appears, Picto shows a sound-alike, and warns you about false friends: pan is bread, not a frying pan.

04 — Culture

Every scene ends with a little of Spain.

After breakfast you learn that pan con tomate is a Spanish classic; at the market, that fruit is sold by the kilo. The words you met go into your word bank, each with its picture and the places you met it.

05 — Made for 40+

Calm, adult and encouraging.

  • No timers, no lives, no shaming. A wrong answer gets a sympathetic look and a hint.
  • Help when you're lost. Tap around and the things you can tap wiggle, then the answer glows, then Glo, a small learning buddy, offers to help.
  • Easy to read and hear. Large type, high contrast and big touch targets. It works with a keyboard alone, and every line can be replayed or slowed down.
06 — How I built it

Every picture is drawn in code.

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework. The scenes, props and people are drawn in code, so they stay sharp and can move: the people come from one rig that breathes, blinks, talks in time with the voice and points when you're stuck. The rules for characters, motion and places are written down, so every new scene follows them.
