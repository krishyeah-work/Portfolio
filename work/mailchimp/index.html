MailChimp on Sales Agent Channel

Helping Intuit sales agents sell MailChimp from the same dashboard they use for QuickBooks.

Overview

A purchase flow for MailChimp inside Intuit's sales dashboard.

What is MailChimp

A marketing platform for small businesses

Owned by Intuit. Businesses buy Email Marketing, then add SMS or Transactional Email.

The requirement

Sell MailChimp through Intuit's sales agents

Customers bought MailChimp online or through a separate sales tool. Intuit wanted its agents to sell it on a live call, from the dashboard they already use.

The solution

A five-step flow, with a clear rule for SMS

I designed the flow for all three products. SMS is charged only after approval, so it gets its own section in the cart.

Success measures

How Intuit will judge the new channel.

  • Channel shareAgent sales made through Intuit's dashboard
  • Revenue gainNew revenue from agent-led sales
  • Time savedHours saved per sale
  • Agent adoptionHow many agents use it, and what they say

01 — The challenge

One cart, two billing timelines.

An order can include three products. Two are paid at checkout. SMS can't be: a business needs an approved application before it can send texts.

  1. Order placed The card is saved and the email products are paid.
  2. SMS application Intuit reviews the application.
  3. Approved SMS is charged on the next bill.
    Not approved SMS is never charged.
Key design challenge

How should SMS sit in the cart when it isn't charged at checkout?

Decision 1

A familiar flow over a simpler new one

Chose
The existing sales flow, with dialogs to add and adjust each product.
Over
The early explorations, which were simpler on their own.
Why
Agents already know it. A one-off flow would need new training and feel out of place. It also fit the timeline and scales to other products.
Final designs

Five steps, from offer to confirmation.

  1. 01Select offerChoose products, tiers and discounts.
  2. 02Customer infoCapture business and contact details.
  3. 03PaymentEnter the card and confirm today's total.
  4. 04Review orderCheck details and record customer consent.
  5. 05ConfirmationOrder placed; SMS awaits approval.

Decision 2

One cart with an approval section, over separate carts

Chose
A dedicated “Upon Application Approval” section inside one cart.
Over
Separate carts, which product managers preferred.
Why
Engineering couldn't build split carts in time. One section keeps SMS apart with a single subtotal, and fits future products that need approval.
Guardrails

Preventing invalid orders before submission.

Add-ons need a base plan, and phase one is for new customers only. Each message says what's wrong and what to do.

05 — Reflection

Team, and what I learned.

Team
  • My contributionResearch, wireframes, cart explorations, final flows, coded prototypes and handoff
  • UX lead and staff product designerDirection and reviews
  • Product managerRequirements, scope and business goals
  • EngineeringFeasibility across order and billing systems
  • LegalBilling consent requirements
  • FinanceDiscount rules
What I learned

  • Alignment is part of the design

    A design moves only when product, engineering, legal and finance agree. I share options early and explain why.

  • Copy is a product decision

    One cart label shapes billing, compliance and trust on every sale. Plain words prevent costly mistakes.

  • Design for the system, not the screen

    The approval section worked because it can hold future products. I now ask early how a decision will scale.
