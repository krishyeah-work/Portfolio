MailChimp on Sales Agent Channel

Enabling Intuit sales agents to sell MailChimp Email Marketing, Transactional Email and SMS from the same sales dashboard they use for QuickBooks.

My role
UX Designer
Team
UX lead, staff product designer, product manager, engineering, legal, finance
Timeline
Jan – Mar 2026
Platform

Overview

A purchase flow for MailChimp inside Intuit's sales dashboard.

What is MailChimp

A marketing platform for small businesses

Owned by Intuit. Businesses subscribe to Email Marketing and can add SMS and Transactional Email.

The requirement

Sell MailChimp through Intuit's sales agents

MailChimp was purchased self-serve or through a separate sales tool. Intuit needed its agents to sell it from their existing dashboard, on the customer's behalf, during a live call.

The solution

A five-step flow, with a clear rule for SMS

I designed the purchase flow for all three products. SMS is charged only after its application is approved, so it sits in a dedicated cart section, separate from the amount charged today.

Configuration and pricing

Contact tier, discount and the full price range sit in one dialog with a live estimated total. In the cart, “How is this calculated?” shows how each price was derived.

Why SMS is different

SMS is only charged once its application is approved.

A business needs an approved application before it can send texts. Intuit handles the paperwork, with the customer's card already on file.

  1. Order placed The card is saved. Email products are charged today.
  2. SMS application Reviewed for approval. Intuit manages the paperwork.
  3. Approved SMS is charged on the next billing cycle.
    Not approved SMS is never charged.

Impact on the cartSMS has a price, but it can't be part of today's total.

Key design challenge

How should SMS appear in the same cart as other products when it is not charged at checkout?

Cart explorations

Separating today's charges from charges after approval.

I explored several cart structures before arriving at a dedicated section for products that require approval. Scroll to see how the cart evolved.

  1. Exploration 1 of 3

    Inline status

    An approval tag on the SMS line kept everything in one list, but left it unclear whether SMS was part of the subtotal.

  2. Exploration 2 of 3

    $0 due today

    Showing $0 clarified today's charge, but suggested a free product, which the requirements did not allow.

  3. Exploration 3 of 3

    Separate carts

    Splitting the order into two carts made the distinction clear, but two subtotals for a single order added complexity. It was also not technically feasible, so it was not taken forward.

  4. Final direction

    A dedicated approval section

    SMS keeps its full price and discount under “Upon Application Approval” and is excluded from today's subtotal. The team aligned on this pattern because it keeps one cart and extends to future products that need approval before billing.

Guardrails

Preventing invalid orders before submission.

Add-ons cannot be purchased without an Email Marketing plan, and phase one supports new customers only. Each message explains the issue and the next step.

Payment and review

Today's charges stay clearly separated through checkout.

Payment and review show “Today's Subtotal” and “Total Due Today”, while SMS remains visible under its approval label through to the order confirmation. Before placing the order, the agent confirms the billing consent script has been read to the customer.

07 — Reflection

What this project taught me.

This was my first project inside an organization of Intuit's scale, and it changed how I think about design work.

  • Alignment is part of the design

    A design moves forward only when product, engineering, legal and finance agree on it. I learned to share options early, explain the reasoning behind each one, and work with PMs and product designers until we reached a decision together.

  • Small decisions carry weight

    A single label in a cart affects billing, compliance and customer trust across every sale made through the channel. I now judge each detail by its effect on the business, not only on the screen.

  • Design for the system, not the screen

    The approval section worked because it could hold future products as well as SMS. Asking how a decision will scale is now one of the first questions I ask.
