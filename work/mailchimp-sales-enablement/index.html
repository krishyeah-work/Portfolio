01 — About Mailchimp

A marketing platform for small businesses, owned by Intuit.

Mailchimp helps businesses reach their customers through email, SMS and websites. Restaurants, entertainment venues, non-profits and online sellers use it to run campaigns, automate communication and measure results.

Email Marketing is the core product. Additional products are purchased as add-ons to it.

Primary product

Email Marketing

Marketing campaigns sent to a contact list, such as a seasonal sale announcement.

Add-ons

SMS

Marketing text messages sent to customers' phone numbers.

In scope

Transactional Email

Automated messages such as receipts and order confirmations.

In scope

Websites

Website creation and publishing.

Out of scope
02 — Current experience

Today, businesses buy Mailchimp on their own.

Businesses choose a plan on Mailchimp's pricing page and complete the purchase themselves. I began by mapping this experience in detail, as each pricing rule would need to carry over to the sales flow.

Source: mailchimp.com, early 2026

03 — Why the sales channel

Intuit's sales agents already speak with small businesses every day.

Agents sell QuickBooks through Intuit's sales dashboard, while Mailchimp was sold through a separate tool with its own pricing. Bringing Mailchimp into the same dashboard allows agents to offer it in every relevant conversation.

Before

Separate tools and pricing

QuickBooks and Mailchimp were sold through different systems, with pricing managed in different places.

→
Goal

One sales dashboard

Agents purchase Mailchimp on the customer's behalf in Intuit's sales tool, with pricing from a single source.

Primary user

Intuit sales agent

Builds the order on the customer's behalf during a live call.

User needs
  • Configure plans and add-ons quickly during the conversation
  • Explain prices, discounts and the timing of each charge
  • Avoid orders that fail after submission

05 — Wireframes

From a single page to a guided flow.

Round 1

All products on a single page

The first concept placed every product on one page, with modifiers for contacts, SMS credits and add-ons alongside a running cart.

Round 2

A dedicated step for adding and modifying products

Configuration moved to its own step, with add-ons grouped under the base plan and a rate table available on demand so agents can explain pricing to the customer.

In high fidelity, configuration moved into a dialog over the offer table. This kept agents in the catalog with the cart in view, rather than sending them to a separate page.

06 — Final designs

Final designs and key decisions.

Offer selection

A familiar catalog, with the cart always in view.

Offers list price ranges, discount terms and billing frequency, consistent with the existing sales flow. The cart remains visible as the order is built.

Configuration and pricing

Every price can be explained to the customer.

Contact tier, discount and the full price range sit in one dialog with a live estimated total. In the cart, “How is this calculated?” shows how each price was derived.

Key design challenge

How should SMS appear in the same cart as other products when it is not charged at checkout?

Cart explorations

Separating today's charges from charges after approval.

I explored several cart structures before arriving at a dedicated section for products that require approval.

The team aligned on this direction because it keeps the order in one cart and extends to future products that require approval before billing.

Guardrails

Preventing invalid orders before submission.

Add-ons cannot be purchased without an Email Marketing plan, and phase one supports new customers only. Each message explains the issue and the next step.

Payment and review

Today's charges stay clearly separated through checkout.

Payment and review show “Today's Subtotal” and “Total Due Today”, while SMS remains visible under its approval label through to the order confirmation. Before placing the order, the agent confirms the billing consent script has been read to the customer.
