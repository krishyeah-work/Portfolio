Case study 01 · Intuit × Mailchimp

One order. Two kinds of charge.

Intuit's sales agents needed to sell Mailchimp to new businesses. One add-on, SMS, can't be billed until it's approved, which can happen after the order is placed. I designed the purchase flow and the cart that makes that clear before anyone pays.

01 — The project

Selling Mailchimp from the tool agents already use.

Intuit owns Mailchimp, but agents sold it in a separate tool from QuickBooks. This project brought Mailchimp into Intuit's own sales tool so one system, with one source of pricing, could sell both.

The first phase targets US businesses that are new to both Intuit and Mailchimp. An agent builds the order on a call and checks out on the customer's behalf.

  • Base plan

    Email Marketing

    Pick a plan and a contact tier, monthly or annual. Required before any add-on.

  • Add-on

    Transactional Email

    Sold in blocks of emails, billed monthly.

  • Add-on

    SMS

    Sold as monthly credits. Charged only after the SMS application is approved.

02 — The problem

The cart had to tell the truth about money it can't charge yet.

The order is paid and closed today. SMS approval comes later. If it's approved, the saved card is charged then; if it's denied, SMS is never charged.

The requirements were strict:

  • Show what SMS will cost if approved, including its discount.
  • Leave it out of today's total.
  • Never show it as $0. That reads as a free product.

An agent should never have to explain away the number on their own screen.

04 — The full flow

Carrying the idea through checkout.

A clear cart isn't enough if checkout contradicts it. I designed the full five-step flow for each product, so the “upon approval” idea holds all the way to confirmation.

  1. 1Select offerPick products, tiers and discounts. The cart builds as you go.
  2. 2Customer infoBusiness details for a brand-new customer.
  3. 3PaymentCard details, plus a note on taxes that apply after approval.
  4. 4Review order“Total due today”, then the consent step.
  5. 5ConfirmationThe order is placed and summarised for the agent.
PaymentTaxes upon approvalSMS is prorated from its approval date, so its tax can't be known today. The payment step says so instead of showing a wrong number.
ReviewConsent before submitThe agent reads a billing consent script to the customer and confirms it before the order can go through, as legal required.
Blocker“Can't add SMS”Add-ons can't be bought alone. If an agent tries, the message says exactly what to add first.
BlockerExisting customersPhase one is for new customers only. The customer info step catches existing accounts early.
05 — How I worked

Three rounds of wireframes, then high fidelity.

  1. Round 1Initial wireframesMapped Mailchimp onto the existing sales flow: offer table, modify product, cart.
  2. Round 2Product selection variantsCompared offer-table layouts and added a read-only rate-card pop-up so agents can explain pricing.
  3. Round 3Final wireframe flowEnd-to-end flow with discount selection, then reviewed with the team before high fidelity.

I worked closely with my UX lead and PM, sharing changes after each review. A few that came out of those rounds:

  • Show the discount range up front, with the maximum discount as a variation.
  • Make prices link to a rate card instead of crowding the table.
  • Keep a subtotal visible until payment, because tax depends on the customer's location.

Legal shaped the consent step, finance set the discount rules, and engineering confirmed what the order and billing systems could support.

06 — Impact & status

Built to grow Mailchimp through Intuit's sales team.

Why it matters

  • A new sales channel for Mailchimp, part of Intuit's FY26 plan to grow it.
  • Mailchimp and QuickBooks sold from one system, with one source of truth for pricing.
  • A cart pattern that works for any product that needs approval before billing.

How success will be measured

  • Share of agent-led Mailchimp sales going through the new flow
  • Revenue gain from agent sales
  • Time saved per sale
  • Agent feedback and adoption
