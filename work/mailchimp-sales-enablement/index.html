Case study 01 · Intuit × Mailchimp

What's charged now, and what's charged only if approved.

Intuit wanted its sales agents to sell Mailchimp to businesses that are new to Intuit. I designed the purchase flow agents use to build that order — including the decision that made add-ons with approval-dependent billing make sense to everyone on the call.

The problem

One cart, two kinds of charge. SMS can't be billed until an outside authority approves it; Email Marketing can be billed right away. A single total would tell the agent — and the customer — something untrue.

My call

I proposed splitting the cart into charged today and charged only if approved. The team aligned on it as a pattern that scales to future approval-dependent products.

Where it is

High-fidelity flows handed off; the feature is in launch and deployment as of October 2026. I don't have usage results to share yet.

01 — Context

A new way to bring Mailchimp to new customers.

Intuit's sales agents talk to businesses every day, including ones that don't use any Intuit product yet. Internally these are NTTF customers: New To The Franchise.

The initiative gave agents a way to sell Mailchimp to those businesses directly from the sales channel, with Email Marketing as the primary product and Mailchimp SMS and Transactional Email as add-ons.

The business bet is simple: every agent conversation with a new business becomes a chance to start a Mailchimp relationship — if the agent can build the order confidently, in one flow, while the customer is still on the call.

02 — Challenge

Some products can be billed today. Some can't be billed until someone else says yes.

Email Marketing can be bought and billed immediately. SMS needs approval from an outside authority before the business can use it. The business consents up front to having its saved card charged for SMS if approval is granted — and isn't charged if it's denied.

So one order holds two clocks. The obvious cart design hides that.

03 — Exploration

I worked with the PM and staff product designer to pin down requirements and scope, then joined the team's solution brainstorms. From there I built mid-fidelity variations of the flow so we could compare approaches side by side instead of debating them in the abstract.

04 — Decision

Split the cart by when money moves.

I proposed separating the order summary into two groups:

  • Charged today — products that can be billed immediately.
  • Charged only if approved — products whose charge depends on a future approval, with the condition stated right next to the amount.

The team aligned on this direction. Part of the reason was that it doesn't only fix SMS: any future product that needs outside approval drops into the second group without redesigning the summary.

An agent should never have to explain away the number on their own screen.

What the split has to communicate, every time:

  • Amount — what's paid today vs. what may be paid later.
  • Condition — what the later charge depends on.
  • Consent — that the saved card will be charged if approval comes through.
  • Denial — that nothing is charged if it doesn't.

06 — Alignment

Billing that depends on consent needs more than design sign-off.

Because the flow touches money and consent, I worked through requirements and feasibility with partners well beyond design, then supported changes and handoff as the feature moved toward launch.
